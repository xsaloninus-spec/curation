## B2 · 文物 02

**弹窗章节副标题**：Mirror in Pre-Qin Period — The start of native tradition and independent development

### 墙上讲解牌

**object.02.name**（name · Slide 6）

> Double-Tier Mirror with Interlaced Dragons

**meta.02**（meta · Slide 6）

> Eastern Zhou, Warring States · Bronze · The Metropolitan Museum of Art

**caption.02**（caption · Slide 6）

> During the Warring States, a native tradition of mirror casting was established in China, and mirrors began to accompany the deceased in tombs, a new trend to provide the dead with the personal articles they had used in daily life. This mirror is built in two tiers: an openwork grille of interlaced dragons set before a plain backing disc.

### 360° 弹窗

**meta**（Slides 5–7）

> Full name: 蟠螭紋銅鏡 · Period: Eastern Zhou, Warring States period · Collection: The Metropolitan Museum of Art, USA · Dimensions: diam. 13.3 cm (5 1/4 in.) · Material: bronze

**caption**

> During the Warring States, the native tradition of mirror casting was established. At the same time, mirrors began to accompany the deceased in tombs, as part of a new trend to provide the dead with personal articles used in daily life.
>
> INHERITANCE — Early mirrors, as subordinate objects, shared design features with bronze ritual vessels, which were usually made in the same workshops. The fine-patterned backgrounds were originally descended from the “thunder pattern” (雷云纹) of squared spirals on the ritual vessels.
>
> INVENTION — In this period, openwork grilles replaced the earlier horse-and-chariot gear buried in tombs, where bronze disks were included. Based on this new technique, craftsmen put grille and mirror together to create the new double-tier mirror.

>
> COMMUNICATIVE MEDIA TOWARD THE COSMOS
>
> REPRESENTING THE COSMIC SCHEME — Han thinkers incorporated former cosmology and developed a mature correlative cosmology. Mirrors represented this kind of cosmic scheme. The TLV pattern, inspired from the diviner’s board (式盤), symbolized the regulation of cosmic order.
>
> RELIGIOUS PRACTICE — As the power of reflection, even if for the hereafter, the mirror could set a man permanently in his correct relation in the cosmic order.
>
> Cosmic correspondences: 圓 — heaven; 方 — earth; 極星 — the pole star; 四獸 — metal, wood, water, fire, yin and yang; 天干地支 — the calendrical signs.

## B4 · 文物 04

**弹窗章节副标题**：Sui & Tang Dynasty — Silk Road Encounters: From Sacred Symbols to Worldly Romance

### 墙上讲解牌

**object.04.name**（name · Slide 12）

> Gold-Backed Beast & Grape Mirror

**meta.04**（meta · Slide 12）

> Tang dynasty, 618–907 · Bronze & gold (repoussé) · Xi’an Museum

**caption.04**（caption · Slide 13）

> This mirror shows how Silk Road trade reshaped Tang visual culture: traditional Chinese auspicious beasts are surrounded by scrolling vines and grape motifs imported from Central and West Asia, and the eight-lobed floral shape breaks the rigid circular tradition of earlier dynasties. Mirrors had shifted from Han cosmological talismans to luxurious, worldly commodities.

### 360° 弹窗

**meta**（Slides 11–14）

> Full name: Gold-Backed Beast&Grape Mirror 金背瑞兽葡萄镜 · Period: Tang dynasty (618–907 CE) · Source: excavated in 2002 from the Tang tomb of Yan Chi and his wife at Majiagou, Baqiao, Xi’an · Current collection: Xi’an Museum · Dimensions: eight-lobed shape · Material: bronze, gold (repoussé 锤揲工艺)

**caption**

> This mirror invites us to see how Silk Road trade reshaped Tang visual culture. At its center, traditional Chinese auspicious beasts are surrounded by scrolling vines and grape motifs — symbols imported from Central and West Asia. The eight-lobed floral shape also breaks the rigid circular tradition of earlier dynasties. In Tang China, mirrors shifted from Han-era cosmological talismans to luxurious, worldly commodities. The exotic decoration reflects not only the elite’s fascination with foreign cultures but also a broader cultural openness, where global trade networks seamlessly integrated with local craftsmanship on a personal object.
>
> GROUP MOTIFS — Auspicious beasts · Eight-lobed form.
>
> Notice the repoussé gold shell (锤揲金背) on the back. Unlike the heavy, dark bronze of the Han dynasty, this gold-backing technique makes the mirror exceptionally luxurious and bright. As sacred and cosmological attributes faded, the mirror became a symbol of earthly prosperity. The Tang elite deliberately embraced these highly decorative, exotic features to display their wealth. It marks the transitional moment when mirrors became a romantic, secular luxury item for daily life, paving the way for the mass markets of the Song dynasty.

### 360° 弹窗

**meta**（Slides 16–19）

> Full name: Mirror with an Immortal Riding a Deer 仙人骑鹿镜 · Period: Song dynasty · Source: britishmuseum.org/collection/object/A_1990-0531-8 · Current collection: The British Museum, London · Dimensions: diameter 10.30 cm; length 18.80 cm (with handle); weight 291 g · Material: bronze

**caption**

> This bronze mirror invites us to see how Daoist ideas entered everyday life. At its centre, an immortal rides a deer, accompanied by a crane, auspicious clouds, and an attendant. The deer and crane are familiar Daoist symbols of longevity and transcendence. Yet the scene is remarkably approachable: faith does not only exist in palaces and Daoist temples, but also integrates into the public’s daily life on the back of the mirror. This is the secularization of Daoist belief in visual form — not an invitation to leave the world for paradise, but a promise of protection, health, and long life carried on a personal object.
>
> NORMALIZATION — Notice the handle at the bottom of the mirror, which turns the sacred image into something that can be held in hand, and brings the mirror closer to commoners, facilitating everyday use.
>
> COMMERCIALIZATION — As ritual attributes faded, commercial thoughts entered the mirror. The craftsman deliberately packaged this approachable deity as a marketable selling point to attract ordinary buyers, reflecting the logic of a mass market.

Here, the mirror is no longer a medium for religious narratives or market brands; it has become a multi-material luxury good, reflecting the global trade networks and luxurious aesthetic of its time.
